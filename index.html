How to use this board

Two jobs, in this order: you shrink 0–9 from the latest official row, then the app turns that tell into tickets so one favourite digit cannot eat the slip. Cash 5 still pays on five whole numbers. Tens are only 0–4. The real work here is the ones digits.

Ending pool

Source is last night’s endings. Example: 15 25 28 30 38 shows as 5 5 8 0 8. That is the input. Nothing here has seen tomorrow.

Chooser picks which digit is the pivot:

  • High — largest ending (8 in that row). Default.
  • Low — smallest ending (0).
  • Tightest — try every unique digit and keep the one whose math makes the smallest pool.
  • 0 + alternate — if 0 is on the row, use 0 plus a partner (often a digit the previous pivot produced, like 2 from 10 − 8). 0 alone just copies the row.
  • Manual — click 1–2 of the digit cards yourself.

Each digit card is a unique ending on that row. Click one or two to set the pivot. The pool is pivot ± every other ending on last night’s row, using the ones digit. Example: high 9 against 6, 7, and 8 is 9±6=3/5, 9±7=2/6, 9±8=1/7, so the pool is 1 2 3 5 6 7. The pivot itself is not added unless an equation produces it. A chip with an amber ring was also on the source row, so it can repeat. The lists of 06 · 16 · 26 · 36 are the full Cash 5 numbers those endings allow.

Equations and extra operator toggles live under Reference if you want the work shown.

Core, Spread, Guard

These three system lines rebuild whenever the pool changes. They are not Quick Picks and not quality ranks.

  • Core plays the strongest tell first (endings with the most live equations, including the pivot).
  • Spread uses the same pool, different tens, no repeated numbers.
  • Guard plays pool digits you are likely to ignore when you lock onto one number. Still inside the pool.

The small digit under each number is the ending. Hover or click a number for the why, such as “17 · ending 7 from 15 − 8.” If you see a dash on Guard or Spread, the pool is so tight there are not 15 legal numbers. The app leaves the line blank rather than grabbing random 1–42. A 3-ending pool often only fills Core and Spread. That is honest.

Show all digits is research (what historically followed this Ball’s ending). It does not pick tickets. Your pick below the board is an extra line if you want one. The three system lines are already the app’s answer.

Whole-number themes

Sometimes the picture is the complete numbers, not the 0–9 pool: a consecutive pair sliding for several draws, a high cluster like 36–39, an exact number coming back under a new identity. When that stacks, the board flashes Whole-number theme live, highlights those numbers in Draw History, and offers a theme line you can drop into Your pick. That is a shape alert, not a jackpot claim. Watch the complete numbers when it fires.

Tonight

  1. Look at Source. That is last night’s endings.
  2. Start with High, or click the digit you actually see as the pivot.
  3. Read Pool. Those endings, and the full numbers under them, are the tell.
  4. Stop. Do not hunt for a prettier 8. That is the “too focused” failure.
  5. Read Core / Spread / Guard. Those are the three slips. Click any number for the why.
  6. Save for the next draw when you are done. Changing the pool updates pending system lines; scored old sessions stay frozen.
